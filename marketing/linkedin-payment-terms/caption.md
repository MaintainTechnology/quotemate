## Caption

```
Australian small businesses are getting paid faster than at any point since Xero
started tracking it in 2017, an average of 24 days, according to their latest
Small Business Insights.

Construction didn't get the memo. CreditorWatch found 92% of construction firms
had an overdue invoice in the last 12 months, with 39% sitting past 30 days.
Highest late-payment rate of any industry in the country.

So the problem isn't the economy. It's structural, and it's specific to how our
industry sells work.

A quote is a document that asks someone to think about it. By the time an invoice
exists, the work is done, the materials are paid for, and every bit of leverage is
gone.

We built QuoteMax so the sequence runs the other way. The customer describes the
job, gets a priced quote back with three options, pays a deposit, then picks a
window in the tradie's calendar. Booking happens after the deposit, not before.

It won't suit every trade. Big commercial work doesn't run that way and never
will. But for domestic jobs, we think asking for commitment before scheduling is
more reasonable than asking for payment 30 days after you've finished.

Curious whether deposit-first works in your trade or kills the sale.

#Construction #Tradies #SmallBusinessAU #CashFlow #PaymentTerms
```

One change to your text: a comma after `2017`. As written, "since Xero started
tracking it in 2017 an average of 24 days" runs two clauses together and reads as
a typo on a post whose whole credibility rests on getting two statistics right.
Nothing else was touched.

Note "leverage" survives here on purpose. It is on the brand's banned-word list,
so it does not appear on any of the six graphics, but this is your post in your
voice and the sentence is doing real work. The graphic version of that line
(QM-2607-69) says "you are asking rather than agreeing" instead.

## How LinkedIn will actually display this

Worth knowing before you post, because it changes what people see.

A **two-image** LinkedIn post renders the pair as side-by-side tiles in the feed,
each centre-cropped. On a 4:5 graphic that removes roughly the outer third of
each side. On image one that clips "OVERDUE INVOICE" off the claim line. Readers
who tap see the full graphic; readers who scroll do not.

Three ways to run it, in the order I would try them:

1. **Two-page document.** Upload `out/quotemax-deposit-first-2pp.pdf` as a
   document post. Both images show in full 4:5, no cropping, and the swipe is a
   real engagement signal. Document title: `Construction gets paid last`.
2. **Single image plus comment.** Post image one on its own, where a 4:5 graphic
   shows uncropped and at maximum feed height. Put image two in your first
   comment with the sources.
3. **The two-image post as briefed.** Accept the crop. Simplest, and the tap-
   through still works.

Option 1 gets you exactly what you asked for, two images and the content, with
nothing cut off. Option 3 is what you asked for literally. Your call.

This display behaviour is general LinkedIn practice, not something measured on
your account. If you already know how your audience behaves, trust that over
this.
