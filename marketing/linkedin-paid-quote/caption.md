## Caption

```
Three tradies quote the same job.

Two of them worked for free.

That is the maths of quoting, and the trade has just accepted it. Drive out,
park, measure up, get in the roof cavity, drive back, write it up that night at
the kitchen table. Then wait. Then hear nothing.

Every other profession charges for the assessment. A vet charges a consult. A
building inspector charges hundreds before they say a word. A conveyancer bills
to read a contract.

Only trades give away the diagnosis and hope to make it back on the job.

So we built the opposite into QuoteMax.

Most jobs never need a visit. The quote gets drafted from the customer's text,
or the roof gets measured off satellite imagery, and it goes back in under a
minute.

But when a job genuinely needs eyes on it, the customer pays $99 to book the
visit. Refundable, and credited straight to the final quote.

Nobody serious blinks at $99. The ones who do blink were never going to sign.

The tradie gets paid for the trip either way.

To be straight, this is built and running in test, not yet collecting real money
at scale. But the position is the point.

Quoting is work. Work gets paid for.

Would you charge for a quote?

#Tradies #Quoting #SmallBusinessAU #Construction
```

## One number to be careful with

`2 IN 3` on graphic 80 is arithmetic off your own premise, three tradies quote
and one wins, not a survey. The source line on the graphic says exactly that in
as many words: **"ARITHMETIC, NOT A SURVEY"**. If anyone reads it as research,
the graphic already answers them. Do not repeat it in the caption as a
statistic.

## About the photograph

The supplied frame is a composite: a tradie in a roof cavity above, an operator
with a tablet reading "PAID BOOKING: $99" below. It was split at the white band
into two photographs, which is what lets the problem sit above the answer.

Two things were handled rather than shipped:

- The tablet's interface text is AI-garbled ("Search fropdiecales", "40,25 mm"
  for a roof area). That frame is never used above 622px wide, where it reads as
  interface rather than as words. The full-bleed graphic (82) uses the roof
  cavity, which carries no text at all.
- A **RELIA-ROOFING** wall logo was cropped out. It is an invented company, and
  next to QuoteMax claims it would read as a named customer, which there is no
  basis to imply. A smaller logo remains on the polo at a size where it reads as
  workwear rather than as a claim.

If anyone asks whether that is your product on screen, the honest answer is that
it is a mock-up, not a QuoteMax screenshot.
