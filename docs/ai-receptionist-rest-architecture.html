As built

The AI Receptionist as it runs today

One shared pipeline serves every trade. A customer text lands on a single route, an LLM runs the conversation, and a chain of engines turns the answers into a priced quote. Every dollar comes from a tool call, never from the model.

Front Door

/api/sms/inbound, the Twilio webhook. Acknowledges in under 500 ms, then works in the background. Holds four trade receptionists today.

Dialog Engine

Sonnet 5 runs the conversation and returns a tool choice, never a price. Deterministic state machines remain the per-turn fallback.

Intake Engine

/api/intake/structure. Opus 4.8 with vision turns free text and photos into a typed job.

Estimation Engine

/api/estimate/draft. Opus 4.8, tool-calling only. Roofing, solar and painting bypass it for pure deterministic pricers.

Evaluator

Checks every line against the pricing book and catalogue. Failure downgrades the quote rather than shipping a guess.

Image Generation

Trade "after" renders. FLUX.1-Kontext primary, Replicate then Gemini as fallbacks. Never touches price.

Why change anything

One route file carries every trade, so a roofing change can break painting. An open roofing thread currently captures later turns whatever the customer asks, which is exactly the class of fault that isolation removes.

Deployed

New architecture: a Front Desk in front of five services

The Front Desk is its own service, not the website: it identifies tenant + trade from the message and the live conversation state, then forwards the turn to that trade's receptionist. Each receptionist is a standalone NestJS app on Railway. The website keeps everything the customer clicks — quote pages, booking, payment, dashboard — reading the same database the receptionists write.

The carve is scripted — and shipped

scripts/export-receptionist.mjs walks the import graph from the three route files, copies the exact closure each trade needs, and writes the NestJS shell. Copied files are byte-identical, so re-running after a change gives a clean diff instead of a merge conflict. Two copy-time transforms were added while wiring the deployed fleet: the simulate channel signs its synthetic webhook with the service's own Twilio token (the route's signature validation has no bypass), and the two engine self-calls are rewritten to ENGINE_BASE_URL ?? APP_URL so each service runs its own intake/estimate engines. (Found live: www.quotemax.com.au 307-redirects to the apex and fetch strips the Authorization header on a cross-host redirect, so a Bearer-authenticated engine call via APP_URL=www.… arrives without its secret and 401s. Loopback is immune.) These script changes are not yet pushed to main.

Deployed · call contract

How the Front Desk talks to a receptionist

One forwarded turn per inbound message, over the signed /api/receptionist/simulate channel (x-sim-key). The Front Desk keeps the Twilio webhook contract and the fast acknowledgement; the receptionist owns the conversation and the price, signs the synthetic webhook internally with its own Twilio token, and replies by SMS. The Front Desk reads that reply back out of the shared sms_messages table.

What isolation buys, stated plainly

Changing roofing cannot break painting. Each trade deploys, rolls back and scales on its own, and its prompt, tools and flags live in one repo. The cost is five deploys to keep in step and a shared database that still needs app-layer tenant scoping.
