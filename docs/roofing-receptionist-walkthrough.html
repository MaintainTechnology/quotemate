The AI Roofing
Receptionist

A customer texts the roofer's normal mobile number. Nobody picks it up. The receptionist does. It asks the right questions, checks the address on the map, measures the roof from satellite, sends a priced quote, and tells the roofer the moment there is a real job. This page walks through exactly what it sends, why it sends it, and where it stops itself.

01

The one-minute version

Five stages. The customer only ever sees a normal text conversation.

It talks like a person

Greetings, questions, corrections, "no worries mate": all handled in natural language, not a rigid menu.

It never invents a number

Every price, area, address and link is produced by code. If the AI writes one, the whole message is thrown away.

It never loses a lead

Anything it can't handle becomes an on-site inspection or an alert to the roofer. It never dead-ends.

02

How it handles one message

Every incoming text runs through the same short loop. The AI decides what to do; code decides what the numbers are.

What the AI is allowed to know

A deliberately tiny fact sheet: business name, owner's first name, the trades they offer, and the state they work in. That is all.

Anything else (years trading, weekend work, licence numbers, insurance, warranty terms) is unknown by construction. It cannot leak what it was never given.

What happens when it doesn't know

It says so honestly, and the roofer is texted straight away so the promise is actually kept.

ReceptionistGood question, I'll check with Ricardo and come back to you. Anything else I can help with in the meantime?
03

The scenario, step by step

Dave texts Ricardo's Roofing at 7:42pm on a Tuesday. Ricardo is at the pub. Here is every message, in order, with what happened behind it.

01

The first message in

Dave's text arrives on Ricardo's own mobile number. The receptionist reads the whole message first. If Dave already gave the address, the job type or the roof material, it takes them and skips those questions.

Dave · 7:42pmHi mate, after a price on a new roof
Receptionist · 7:42pmHappy to sort a roofing quote for you. What's the property address, including suburb and postcode?
Behind the scenes
  • "new roof" is a roofing enquiry, so the roofing receptionist takes the thread. For a roofing-only business it takes every thread, keyword or not.
  • If several texts land within a second or two, they are treated as one message so nothing is dropped.
  • Nothing was volunteered here, so it asks the first missing thing: the address.
02

The address is checked on the map, twice

Dave's typing is never trusted on its own. Before the address is read back, it is checked against two independent registers. Reading a customer's own typo back to them just gets a "yep".

Dave670 London Road Chandler 4155
ReceptionistJust to confirm, the property is "670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155". Is that right? Reply yes or no.
Daveyep that's it
Behind the scenes
  • A real case: a customer typed "15 Schofield drive safety each", where "each" was meant to be "beach". The old read-back parroted it and they said yes. The map check catches that class of error.
  • Google alone is not enough: it quietly echoes a suburb it could not confirm. Geoscape is the register the measurement itself uses, so agreeing with Geoscape means the address confirmed is the address measured.
  • If both services are down, the plain read-back still goes out. Verification is a safety net, never a gate. An outage must not block a lead.
  • "yep that's it" is accepted as yes. So are "y", "yeah", "sure", "no worries", "👍".
03

Three job questions, asked like a person

It needs exactly three more things before it can price: what the job is, what the roof is made of, and how steep it is. It asks one at a time, and it accepts answers in any order.

ReceptionistWhat do you need done? A full re-roof, a repair or patch, a leak traced, or gutters and downpipes?
Davefull reroof. it's colorbond
ReceptionistRighto - which Colorbond profile is it? Corrugated (the classic wavy sheets) or Trimdek (flat panels with square ribs)? If you're not sure, just say so and we'll check it on site.
Davecorrugated
ReceptionistRoughly how steep is the roof? Flat, standard, or steep?
Davestandard i reckon
Behind the scenes
  • Dave answered two questions in one message. The material was picked up out of order and the material question was never asked.
  • "Colorbond" alone doesn't price a job (the profile changes the sheet cost), so it asks which rather than guessing.
  • Some answers stop the quote early and route straight to an on-site visit: fibro or cement sheet (possible asbestos), an unknown material, a very steep or unknown pitch, an unclear job, or a commercial building.
  • If the same question is answered unclearly two or three times, it stops asking and routes to an inspection instead of looping. No question can be asked forever.
04

It measures the roof and shows Dave the photos

The brief is complete, so the measurement runs on satellite imagery and the property register, with no AI involved. Then it sends the roof photos and asks Dave to confirm the buildings before quoting.

📷 Photo sent · "Main dwelling"
📷 Photo sent · "Secondary structure 1"
📷 Photo sent · "Secondary structure 2"
ReceptionistHi Dave, I found 3 buildings at 670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155 (I've sent photos to check): 1) Main dwelling (~248 m²) 2) Secondary structure 1 (~64 m²) 3) Secondary structure 2 (~31 m²) Reply YES to quote all of them, the number for just one, or NO if none are right. See them here too: quotemax.com.au/q/roof/ab12cd34?pick=1
Behind the scenes
  • Nothing is priced to the customer until they confirm which buildings are theirs. Sheds, garages and granny flats are all found separately.
  • Dave can reply YES (all), 2, 2 and 3, the shed, just the big one, Main or NO. All understood.
  • NO means wrong building, so it clears the address and starts again: "No worries. What's the correct property address, with suburb and postcode?"
  • The confirm link ends in ?pick=1 so re-opening it still shows the picker, not a narrowed view.
05

Dave says yes, and the quote goes out

Every figure in this message came out of the pricing engine untouched. The AI did not write one character of it.

Daveyes all of them
ReceptionistHi Dave, here's your roofing estimate for 670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155 (3 structures, ~343 m² total): • Full roof replacement: $69,652 Full breakdown + your roof image: quotemax.com.au/q/roof/ab12cd34 PDF copy: quotemax.com.au/q/roof/ab12cd34/pdf Prices inc GST. A roofer reviews every quote before we book anything.
Behind the scenes
  • The confirmed buildings are written to the job record, not just onto the link, so the SMS total and the web page can never disagree.
  • A PDF is generated and linked. Later picks ("actually just 2 and 3") regenerate it so the document always matches the text.
  • Some roofers show one price, others show three tiers. That is a per-roofer setting, and the SMS follows whatever the web page shows.
  • If solar panels were detected, the detach-and-reinstate allowance is already inside the replacement price.
  • The thread stays warm. Dave can text "what about just the shed?" hours later and get that priced from the same saved measurement.
06

Ricardo's phone buzzes

The moment the quote lands with Dave, Ricardo is told on his own mobile, from his own business number.

To Ricardo · the rooferHi Ricardo - roofing quote sent via SMS at $69,652 inc GST. Customer: Dave (+61 412 345 678) Property: 670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155 Review: quotemax.com.au/q/roof/ab12cd34
Behind the scenes
  • Before this existed, leads sat in the database and nobody was told until someone opened the dashboard.
  • The alert can never break the customer's message. It runs after, and a failure to send it is logged, not thrown.
  • If the "customer" is Ricardo's own handset (he's testing), the alert is suppressed and the reason is logged, so it doesn't look broken.
07

Dave goes quiet, and gets exactly one check-in

Dave reads the quote and puts his phone down. Two hours later, one nudge goes out. Exactly one.

Receptionist · 2 hours laterHi Dave, just checking in on the quote we sent through - did we answer everything, or anything else we can help with? - Ricardos Roofing

If Dave had gone quiet mid-conversation, before any quote existed, he'd get the other wording instead:

Receptionist · mid-intake versionHi Dave, just checking in - still keen to get that job sorted? Reply here and we'll pick up where we left off. - Ricardos Roofing
Behind the scenes
  • Two separate sweeps, and they never double-text: one covers quotes already sent, the other covers conversations that stalled before a quote.
  • One check-in per conversation, ever. Stamped on the record, so a re-run of the sweep cannot send a second.
  • Both are opt-in per roofer, and default to off.
08

Dave pays, picks a time, and Ricardo is booked

Payment comes before the calendar, always. A time can only be picked once the deposit is in.

To DaveHi Dave, you're locked in for Thu 7 May, 9:00am. The tradie will text the day before to confirm. View booking: quotemax.com.au/q/roof/ab12cd34 - QuoteMax
To Ricardo · the rooferHi Ricardo - roofing job BOOKED via SMS for Thu 7 May, 9:00am. Customer: Dave (+61 412 345 678) Property: 670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155 Quoted: $69,652 inc GST (deposit paid) Details: quotemax.com.au/q/roof/ab12cd34
Behind the scenes
  • Both texts use the same clock and the same formatter, in the roofer's own state timezone, so they can never name different days for the same slot.
  • The two messages are sent independently. If Dave's confirmation fails, Ricardo's still goes out, and vice versa. The booking itself is already committed either way.
  • Until recently a paid, booked roofing job reached nobody. That gap is closed.
04

When the conversation goes sideways

The happy path is the easy part. These are the branches that keep a bad turn from costing a lead.

Branch A · the address isn't real

Dave types a suburb that doesn't exist.

Dave670 London Rd, Corparoo 4155
ReceptionistI can't find "670 London Rd, Corparoo 4155" on the map. Did you mean "670 London Rd, Coorparoo QLD 4151"? Reply yes if that's the one, or send the correct address.
Daveno it's chandler
ReceptionistJust to confirm, the property is "670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155". Is that right? Reply yes or no.

And if it still can't be matched after two goes, it stops asking and hands over. It will never measure an address the register can't confirm:

ReceptionistThanks for that. I still can't match "..." on the map, so I'll get one of the team to confirm the property with you and sort your quote from there.
To RicardoHi Ricardo - a customer asked something the SMS receptionist could not answer. Customer: Dave (+61 412 345 678) They asked: Could not match this address on the map: "..." We told them you would come back to them.

Branch B · the roof can't be quoted from the sky

Dave says the roof is fibro. It may contain asbestos, so it is never auto-priced.

Daveit's the old fibro stuff
ReceptionistThanks Dave. Because cement sheet or fibro roofs may contain asbestos, we'll arrange a quick on-site inspection of 670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155 to quote it accurately. Reply YES and we'll book a time that suits you.
Daveyeah go on then
ReceptionistGreat Dave. A roofer will be in touch shortly to lock in a time for the inspection.
To RicardoHi Ricardo - new roofing INSPECTION booked via SMS. Customer: Dave (+61 412 345 678) Property: 670 London Rd, Chandler QLD 4155 Details: quotemax.com.au/q/roof/ab12cd34 Reply to the customer to lock in a time.

Where the roof can be measured but still needs a look, the customer sees a real indicative range clearly labelled "confirmed on site". Never a blank or a $0.

Branch C · a vague answer at the booking question

"Shall we book?" gets a reply that is neither yes nor no.

Davehey mate
ReceptionistJust so I've got it right - would you like us to book the on-site inspection? Reply YES to book, or NO if you'd rather not.
Davewhat's it gonna cost me
ReceptionistGreat Dave. A roofer will be in touch shortly to lock in a time for the inspection.

A greeting is never consent, so that gets a re-ask. But it only re-asks once. A second unclear reply is still a live customer, so it books and alerts the roofer rather than dropping them. Only an explicit no closes it.

Branch D · the customer wants out

STOP is decided by code before the AI is even called, so it costs nothing and can never be second-guessed.

Davenah forget it, not interested
ReceptionistNo problem Dave. I've stopped there. Just text me anytime if you'd like a roofing quote.

And a refusal of the trade is remembered for the whole conversation. If Dave says "no I don't want a roofer", roofing is never raised again on that thread, even though the word "roofer" is right there in his message.

Staying safe and honest

BehaviourWhat it means in practice
STOP decided by codeOpt-out is checked before the AI is called. Compliance is never a judgement call.
Tiny fact sheetBusiness name, owner's first name, trades, state. Licence numbers, ABN, insurance and contact details are not given to the model at all.
No invented factsTrading history, weekend availability, warranties, who owns QuoteMax: all deflected, never guessed.

06

Everything the roofer receives

Four alerts, four triggers. All to their own mobile, all from their own business number, none of them able to break a customer message.

And a page of their own

Every measured job also mints a private roofer link. On it they can switch buildings on and off, correct anything the satellite got wrong, re-price instantly, and save the result as a formal quote. The customer's link and the roofer's link are created as a pair. A job can never exist with only one of them.

07

What it will never do

Worth saying plainly, because these are design decisions, not gaps.

×
Make up a price. Not even a ballpark. Every figure is produced by the pricing engine or it is not sent.
×
Quote an address it can't find. The lead goes to a human instead.
×
Price a possible-asbestos roof from the sky. Fibro and cement sheet always go on site.
×
Auto-quote a commercial building. Residential rates don't apply, so it books a visit.
×
Claim a booking that didn't happen. Confirmations only come from real records.
×
Answer a question about the business it wasn't told. It deflects and escalates.
×
Keep texting after STOP. Decided by code, before the AI runs.
×
Send a second check-in. One per conversation, ever.
The one line to remember

The AI owns the conversation. Code owns the money. Neither can do the other's job, and that is checked on every single message, not trusted.
