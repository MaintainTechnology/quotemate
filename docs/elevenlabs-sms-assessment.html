Architecture assessment · QuoteMax

Can ElevenLabs replace the QuoteMax SMS receptionist?

9 August 2026 Scope: SMS intake, 8 trades, 8 tenants Measured against: monorepo route + Railway fleet

Verdict

Yes for the conversation. No for the money. ElevenLabs now runs SMS as a first-class channel, and adopting it would delete most of the code currently producing your errors. But it has no seam to veto what its model writes, and it cannot send or receive MMS. Both problems share one fix: let ElevenLabs own the dialogue, and keep every message carrying a number or an image coming from your own service.

Recommended: a hybrid pilot on one trade with one tenant. Not a full replacement, and not a rejection.

The three findings that decide it

What the research established

Confirmed

SMS is a real channel, not a workaround

ElevenAgents shipped SMS as a generally available channel on 28 July 2026, alongside Telegram, Intercom and Freshdesk (those three are Alpha; SMS is not). You import a Twilio number that supports SMS, re-import it to enable messaging, then assign an agent. Inbound routing and the outbound reply are both handled by ElevenLabs. You write no webhook.

Blocking

No MMS on the SMS channel

File inputs, meaning images and PDFs, are supported on web, mobile and WhatsApp. SMS is excluded. Your roofing flow texts per-building roof photos, the funnel sends quote PDFs as attachments, and inbound customer photos feed the vision intake. None of that survives a straight port.

Blocking

No hook to veto an ungrounded reply

The platform's model speaks and the platform sends. There is no interception point between the two. Your central invariant, LLM conversation, deterministic money, is enforced by inspecting outbound text and discarding it. That check cannot exist inside ElevenLabs.

Baseline

What you would be replacing

14,510production lines in lib/sms, across 43 modules
4,618lines in the single /api/sms/inbound route
13,270test lines across 62 spec files guarding it

The fleet, qm-front-desk plus five trade receptionists, exists for one structural reason: a Twilio number can point at exactly one URL, so a multi-trade tenant needs a router in front of it. The front desk asks Claude Sonnet 5 to classify every turn at a measured 1.5–3.8 s (median 2.7 s), because 65% of inbound messages carry no trade word at all. ElevenLabs' workflow subagents and agent-transfer tool make that entire service unnecessary.

The blockers in detail

Why a straight replacement fails

Blocker

The grounding validator has no equivalent, and it is the load-bearing safety property

assertGroundedReply rejects a model turn writing a dollar sign, a money word, a percentage, a spelled-out amount, or a number in a price context. It NFKC-normalises first, so a full-width ４５０ cannot walk past the numeric checks, and it grounds by category: an area is grounded only by a previously stated area, a count only by a count. A postcode of 4155 therefore cannot ground "your roof is 4155 sqm". That is deliberately paranoid code, and it is the reason an LLM is allowed to drive your customer conversations at all.

ElevenLabs offers no post-generation, pre-send hook. Workflows constrain when an agent may speak about price, since a dispatch-tool node can force the pricing call to succeed first, but not what it says once it is speaking. A hallucinated "around $4,500" in a chatty turn reaches a real customer with nothing standing in front of it.

Blocker

MMS is not on the SMS channel, and your roofing funnel depends on it

After measureAndPriceRoofs returns, the flow texts one image per detected building and asks which one is theirs. That step is the disambiguation; without the pictures the question is unanswerable. The quote PDF is likewise an attachment, and inbound photos feed the vision intake for painting and signage.

A link-instead-of-attach workaround exists: host the images on the quote page and text the URL. It works. It is also a real product change, and it deserves to be decided on its own merits rather than adopted silently because a platform forced it.

Blocker

You trade your bugs for someone else's, and lose the ability to fix them

Today a routing bug is a twenty-minute fix, with 62 spec files proving you did not break the other seven trades. On a managed platform the same bug becomes a support ticket. Your send-reliability retry ladder, your inflight lock and your grounding check all turn into another company's uptime commitment.

This is not an argument against the move. It is an argument for keeping the money path, the part where being wrong costs a customer real dollars, inside code you own.

The fair case for it

What ElevenLabs would genuinely fix

Your stated complaint is errors and inconsistencies, and most of the documented ones live in code that would simply cease to exist:

  • The roofing hijack. shouldEngageRoofing resumes on an active thread alone and never inspects the inbound text, so on a multi-trade tenant an open roofing thread captures every later turn. Agent transfer between workflow subagents is a first-class primitive, so you would not be hand-rolling this at all.
  • The parsing failures. y, 👍 and ya not read as yes, "no worries" parsed as no, "1 and 2" truncated to a single pick. These are keyword-parser bugs in the deterministic fallback net, and a model handles them natively.
  • The concurrency race. A 60 s inflight lock against a worst-case 200–300 s turn lets a second webhook run concurrently and produce duplicate or out-of-order replies. Session management stops being yours.
  • The fleet's whole reason for existing. One Twilio number, one URL, therefore a router. Gone.
  • Volume. Roughly 19,000 lines of dialogue orchestration and its tests reduce to one agent configuration plus a set of webhook tool definitions.

Recommendation

Split it at the message, not at the system

The MMS problem and the grounding problem share one solution: any message containing a price or an image is sent by your service, never by ElevenLabs. The agent handles conversation. The moment a number is involved, the webhook tool does the sending and returns only a confirmation the agent may repeat.

If you proceed

Pilot sequence

  1. Pick one single-trade tenant

    A roofing-only tenant is the hardest case: address verification, measurement, building disambiguation, MMS. If the hybrid holds there it holds everywhere. Three of your eight tenants are roofing-only.

  2. Expose the existing engines as webhook tools

    The Railway services already speak HTTP with Bearer auth. verify_address, measure_and_price_roof, send_saved_quote and book_inspection become four tool definitions. No new backend code.

  3. Build the workflow graph, not a prompt

    Address, verify, confirm, material, pitch, measure, send. Dispatch-tool nodes with explicit failure paths, expression conditions wherever the rule is deterministic, LLM conditions only where judgement is genuinely required.

  4. Move the priced send into the tool

    The tool sends the SMS, the roof photos and the PDF on your Twilio account, then returns a confirmation carrying no figures. This is the step preserving the money invariant. Do not skip it to save a round trip.

  5. Run both in parallel on a test number first

    Your existing /api/receptionist/simulate channel already replays real turns through the real pipeline. Compare the two receptionists on the same transcripts before a single tenant number moves.

  6. Repoint one number, watch it, then decide about the rest

    Per-number cutover with someone watching, the same discipline the fleet spec already puts on this step.

Cost and open questions

What to confirm before committing

Published agent pricing is $0.08 to $0.12 per minute for voice, with additional text messages quoted at $0.003 each in third-party pricing analyses. At your current volume of 2,597 messages in sms_messages, messaging cost is not a deciding factor either way. The questions below are the ones that matter, and none are answered in the public documentation:

  • SMS channel billing. Per message, per conversation, or per minute of session? The per-message figure comes from pricing write-ups, not from ElevenLabs' own docs.
  • Session lifetime on SMS. Your await_booking state never expires, so a three-day-late "yes book it" still has to reach the right flow. Ask what happens to an ElevenLabs conversation after hours or days of silence.
  • Webhook tool timeout. A roofing measure can take 200 to 300 seconds. If the tool timeout is 30 seconds you need an async pattern, and the agent needs something to say while it waits.
  • Multi-tenancy. Eight tenants with different pricing books and service offerings. Confirm whether dynamic variables at conversation initiation can carry tenant context into one shared agent, or whether you need an agent per tenant.
  • Any pre-send hook at all. Worth asking directly. If ElevenLabs will let you inspect and reject an outbound message, the calculus changes materially and a fuller migration becomes defensible.
