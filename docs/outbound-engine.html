00 · The stack, decided

One pick per node — and what it replaced

You named Clay, ZoomInfo, Apollo and Sales Navigator. The research says those are the wrong instruments for a market of sole traders with no LinkedIn footprint and consumer email addresses. Here is the stack that fits, with the reasoning compressed; the full diagrams and evidence follow.

Lead sources
Outscraper (Google Maps) + state licence registers + Apify (hipages, Yellow Pages AU)

Google Maps is the tradie's real storefront — near-universal phone coverage at ~US$3 per 1,000 places. State licence registers are free and give regulated trade classification no database has. hipages listees already pay for leads, which is exactly QuoteMax's pitch. Replaces: Apollo, ZoomInfo, Sales Navigator (all three are LinkedIn-graph tools aimed at an audience that isn't on LinkedIn).

Enrichment
ABN Lookup (free) → Firecrawl + Claude → Hunter (domain-gated) → thin backups

For sole traders the ABN legal name is the owner's name — free owner enrichment for the biggest segment. Tradie emails live on their own websites, not in contact graphs. Replaces: Clay as the waterfall (its API tier is US$495/mo and its 75+ providers are mostly LinkedIn-shaped — you rebuild the 3–4 rungs that actually hit, in n8n, for a fraction).

Verification
MillionVerifier first pass → ZeroBounce / Bouncer on the catch-all bucket only

Cheapest credible verifier (~US$37 per 10,000, catch-alls not charged) with a scored second opinion on the 8–15% that sit behind catch-all servers. Replaces: NeverBounce (2× the price, same catch-all blindness) and Emailable (fast, but weak on the one failure mode that matters here).

Cold email
Smartlead Pro (US$94/mo) sending through your own Google Workspace inboxes

Connects your Gmail accounts by OAuth — sends genuinely leave your inboxes, satisfying the "from my Gmail" requirement — while Smartlead handles warmup, rotation, throttling, reply detection and webhooks. Replaces: raw Gmail API sending (an account-suspension pattern Google polices) and lemlist (per-seat pricing, 5-inbox cap, LinkedIn features your ICP can't see).

Orchestrator
n8n, self-hosted on Railway (~A$8–23/mo all-in)

Free software, unlimited executions, native HTTP/Postgres/Sheets/Gmail nodes. Replaces: Make.com — its per-operation meter bills every enrichment rung and retry, landing at roughly US$80–110/mo at 10,000 leads × 8 steps and scaling linearly with exactly the thing you're growing.

System of record
Supabase Postgres — already in your stack — with an optional Airtable mirror

250,000 lead rows are trivial for Postgres; unique indexes give real dedupe and a suppression table with foreign keys beats any spreadsheet filter. Replaces: Airtable as master store (50k-record cap on Team, 5 requests/sec API) and Google Sheets (10M-cell ceiling, no constraints, silent corruption at scale). Airtable survives as a marketing-facing mirror of the active subset if you want kanban views without building UI.

01 · Three facts that shape everything

Read these before the diagrams

Market coverage

Your shortlist can't see your market

Apollo's AU data is LinkedIn-derived and Sydney/Melbourne-centric — roughly 40–45% of its AU records fail verification. ZoomInfo runs US$15k–45k+/yr with near non-existent coverage outside major metros. Sales Navigator searches a graph regional sparkies simply aren't on. All three: avoid for this ICP.

Law of the land

Australia is consent-first

The Spam Act 2003 has no B2B exemption — unlike US CAN-SPAM, sending without express or inferred consent is itself the offence, and address-harvesting software is separately banned. Repeat corporate offenders face up to A$3.64M per day of contravention from July 2026. Consent evidence becomes a database column, not a footnote.

Channel reality

Tradies live on the phone

30–50% of tradies have no website, so no findable email — but their Google Maps listing number is almost always an 04xx mobile. Email is the v1 channel; the schema captures and E.164-normalises mobiles from day one so SMS (you already run Twilio AU) is a config change, not a rebuild.

03 · Stage 1 — Lead generation

Source where the ICP actually lives

The Australian tradie TAM is finite — a few hundred thousand licensed trade businesses; the NSW, QLD and VIC registers alone cover most of it. Two or three full passes saturate the market, so the pipeline is built for freshness and dedupe cycles, not infinite net-new volume. Sourcing is designed around published business contact points (the Spam Act's inferred-consent path), never bulk-harvested email lists.

07 · The legal layer

Spam Act 2003 — designed in, not bolted on

The three conditions of every send

  1. Consent. Express, or inferred via the conspicuously-published-address exception — lawful only when all three hold: the address is conspicuously published (their website, a directory, a professional listing); the publication carries no "no unsolicited messages" statement; and the message is directly relevant to the recipient's business role. QuoteMax pitching quoting software to a published trade-business contact address plausibly qualifies — but only with per-address evidence. A gmail address published on the tradie's own site can qualify; the same address exported from a data broker cannot.
  2. Identification. Accurate sender identity — real trading name, ABN and contact details valid for 30 days — in every footer. Lookalike domains must read as QuoteMax and redirect to the real site, never as anyone else.
  3. Unsubscribe. Functional for 30 days after send, at no more than nominal cost, honoured within 5 business days. Broken unsubscribes are ACMA's most common finding. Smartlead's unsubscribe webhook → Supabase suppression table → checked pre-send by the gate, permanently.

Separately, Spam Act ss 20–22 ban address-harvesting software and lists built with it — bulk-scraped or purchased email lists carry their own liability before a single send. This is why sourcing targets published business contact points and why the lead table carries consent_basis, source_url, captured_at and relevance_note as first-class columns: ACMA investigations open with "prove consent for these addresses". Enforcement is live — over A$12M in penalties in ~18 months (Tabcorp >A$4M; historic markers: CommBank A$3.55M, DoorDash A$2M, Kmart A$1.3M). Privacy Act 1988 applies too: a sole trader's business contact details are personal information — keep a collection notice, a deletion path, and real security on the store.

10 · What makes it survive

The unglamorous parts, specified up front

An adversarial review of this plan surfaced the failure modes that kill outbound systems in practice. Each one is a design requirement, not a later nice-to-have.

Existing-relationship exclusion
One SQL join against tenants, Clerk users, trials, signup intents and open conversations. Cold-emailing a paying tenant is a guaranteed embarrassment; the gate blocks it structurally.
Unified suppression
One table covering email + SMS, all three domains, every campaign — address-level AND entity-level (ABN / phone / domain). Free-text opt-outs from replies land here too. Permanent, checked pre-send, never re-imported.
Idempotency everywhere
Idempotency-key every push into Smartlead; DB unique indexes reject duplicates a retry loop creates. An automation bug without this becomes a Spam Act breach at machine speed.
Monitoring + kill switch
Watch bounce rate, complaint rate (Google Postmaster Tools), DMARC aggregates, blacklists, n8n failures, actor schema drift, send-volume anomalies. One global pause control halts all sending when complaints spike.
Lead scoring for a finite TAM
~150 sends/day against a few hundred thousand businesses means ordering is everything: GST-registered, review count, hipages presence, website recency, licence class match. Work the market best-first.
Coarse, sequential testing
At this volume subject-line micro-tests never reach significance. Test angle-level per trade, sequentially, with defined promote/kill rules.
Data hygiene cadence
Weekly ABR refresh deactivates dead ABNs; quarterly re-verification of aging emails (bigpond-class decay faster); bounce-driven auto-cleanup; a re-contact cooldown policy before any second pass over the TAM.
Review queue with an owner
Low-confidence ABN matches and first-batch email QA go to a human queue — wrong firmographics attached to outreach in a small, talkative trade community burns the market permanently.
Per-send audit log
Every send records its consent basis, source URL and capture date — the exact artefact an ACMA "prove consent for these addresses" request demands.
SMS as the designed second act
The research is blunt: this ICP answers the phone, not email. The same leads, consent rules and suppression table serve an SMS channel on your existing Twilio stack — with its own STOP handling, kept separate from the product's transactional STOP logic.
